What Is Gum Disease and What Are the Warning Signs?

Recognizing gingivitis and periodontitis symptoms: bleeding gums, recession, mobility, and professional periodontal treatment.

RC
Redesign Dental Clinics
Clinical Editorial Team • Banjara Hills, Hyderabad

Periodontal (gum) disease is an inflammatory condition triggered by bacterial plaque accumulation along and below the gumline. If left untreated, it can destroy the supporting bone structure holding teeth in place.

Key Clinical Takeaways

Gum disease is preventable and treatable in its early stages; never ignore bleeding gums, as early intervention protects natural tooth support.

The Progression: From Gingivitis to Periodontitis

Gingivitis represents the initial, reversible stage marked by red, swollen gums that bleed during brushing. When plaque calcifies into hardened calculus (tartar), inflammation advances to periodontitis, destroying periodontal ligaments and alveolar bone.

Key Warning Signs Identified by NIDCR

According to the National Institute of Dental and Craniofacial Research (NIDCR), key warning signs include persistent bleeding when brushing or flossing, gum recession, chronic bad breath (halitosis), deep gum pockets, and shifting or loose teeth.

Systemic Health Links

Chronic periodontal inflammation allows oral bacteria to enter the bloodstream. Clinical studies demonstrate associations between severe periodontitis and systemic conditions such as diabetes, cardiovascular disease, and adverse pregnancy outcomes.

Professional Periodontal Treatments

Non-surgical scaling and root planing (deep cleaning) removes subgingival bacterial biofilm and smoothens root surfaces. Advanced cases may benefit from antimicrobial irrigation, laser therapy, or regenerative gum procedures.
