Wisdom Tooth Removal: When Might It Be Recommended?

Understanding third molar impaction, common symptoms like pericoronitis, 3D diagnostics, and surgical recovery guidelines.

RC
Redesign Dental Clinics
Clinical Editorial Team • Banjara Hills, Hyderabad

Wisdom teeth (third molars) typically erupt in late adolescence or early adulthood. When there is insufficient space in the dental arch, these teeth can become impacted, leading to pain, infection, or damage to adjacent molars.

Key Clinical Takeaways

Not every wisdom tooth requires removal, but routine radiographic monitoring ensures problematic impactions are managed before complications arise.

What Causes Wisdom Teeth to Become Impacted?

Impaction occurs when third molars are blocked by bone or overlying gum tissue, erupting at odd angles (mesial, horizontal, or distal). This makes them virtually impossible to clean effectively, allowing bacteria to flourish.

Common Clinical Reasons for Removal

Extraction is recommended for recurrent gum infection around partially erupted crowns (pericoronitis), localized tooth decay, root resorption on the adjacent second molar, cyst formation, or chronic jaw pain.

Diagnostic Imaging: 3D CBCT & Digital OPG

Panoramic OPG radiographs and 3D CBCT scans map the precise proximity of lower wisdom tooth roots to the inferior alveolar nerve canal, ensuring surgical safety and planning.

Post-Operative Recovery and Healing

Following extraction, rest, applying cold compresses, adhering to a soft diet, avoiding straw use (to prevent dry socket), and gentle salt-water rinses promote rapid healing.
